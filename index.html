01 — The unit of analysis

A fixed six-instance rotation

Every result in this document rests on one assumption: the combo is a fixed sequence, not a sustained DPS loop. Quinn fires six damage instances inside a single ~3-second window, and both abilities cancel her auto-attack timer so no swing is wasted.

The sequence — Q-blind → AA(Harrier) → AA → AA → E → AA(Harrier) — produces four basic attacks, two ability hits, and two Harrier (Vulnerable) procs. Because Vault resets the attack timer and Blinding Assault animation-cancels, the four autos genuinely land in-window. This fixed-count structure is what makes a closed-form solution possible — and it is also why attack speed behaves discontinuously, as we'll see.

Modeling premise

Ability cooldowns (Q ≈ 10.5s, E 8–12s) far exceed the 3s window, so each fires exactly once. They are fixed contributions. Only the auto-attack count responds to attack speed — this single fact drives the entire optimization.

Critical asymmetry

Three components never crit: the Harrier proc H(L) (on-hit damage), Q, and E (abilities). Crit multiplies only the auto-attack AD block. On a combo that is ~60% non-critable early, this caps crit's leverage — the central finding of §05.

03 — Closed form

The master equation

Because every component of Quinn's combo is physical, a single mitigation factor governs the entire burst. This is unusual — and it makes the whole system collapse into one clean expression.

Post-mitigation burst damage
$$ D = \Big[\, \underbrace{N(s)\big[\,BaseAD(L)+A\,\big]\,M(c,g)}_{\text{autos — critable}} \;+\; \underbrace{1.05\,A + 140}_{Q+E} \;+\; \underbrace{2\big[\,H(L)+0.40A\,\big]}_{\text{Harrier — on-hit}} \,\Big]\cdot \frac{100}{100+R_{\text{eff}}} $$

The bracketed quantity is raw damage; the trailing factor is the wiki's armor mitigation. Three structural facts fall out immediately:

Insight 01 — Penetration is a global multiplier

Since all damage is physical, 100/(100+R_eff) scales autos, abilities, and procs identically. On a mixed-damage champion you'd need two mitigation terms; Quinn needs one. This rewards committing to physical penetration rather than hedging.

Insight 02 — Crit only touches one term

The multiplier M(c,g) attaches to the auto block alone. Crit and attack speed therefore compound (more autos × bigger crits), while crit does nothing for the ability/proc majority of an early combo.

Collapsing the AD-dependent terms gives the form used for gold-efficiency work, where the coefficient on bonus AD is 1.85 + N(s)·M — the 1.85 being Q (0.85) + E (0.20) + two Harrier procs (0.80):

AD-collapsed form (pre-mitigation)
$$ D_{\text{raw}} = \big(140 + N\cdot BaseAD(L) + 2H(L)\big) \;+\; \big(1.85 + N\cdot M\big)\,A $$
04 — Attack speed dynamics

The staircase: why attack speed is lumpy

In a fixed-window combo, attack speed adds nothing until it crosses a whole-auto threshold — then it jumps by one entire auto. The damage-vs-AS relationship is a step function, not a smooth curve.

Autos in the window are N(s) = ⌊3 · 0.668 · (1+s)⌋ + 1. At level-appropriate base attack speed (0.668), you land 3 autos and don't reach a 4th until total AS hits 1.0 — that is +50% bonus AS for a single extra swing. Between thresholds, every point of AS is wasted on burst.

Insight 03 — AS is a breakpoint purchase

Attack speed is not a smooth stat for burst. It is worthless across each tread and valuable only at the riser. Buy it only when one item pushes 3·totalAS across an integer. Quinn's Heightened Senses steroid (+28–80% AS) typically supplies the first breakpoint for free — meaning purchased AS double-dips poorly.

05 — Critical strike

Crit is a win-more, scaling stat

Crit multiplies only the auto block. Early, that block is a minority of the combo, so crit's leverage is weak. Late — with high base AD, more autos, and a crit-damage item — the auto block dominates and crit compounds.

Setting crit's marginal gold value equal to AD's yields the crossover condition:

Crit overtakes AD when
$$ \frac{N\,(BaseAD(L)+A)\,(1+g)}{40} \;>\; \frac{1.85 + N}{35} $$

At L3, no crit-damage item: AD wins ≈2.4× (0.139 vs 0.057 dmg/gold). At L16 with an IE-tier item (g = 0.40, N = 4, A = 60): crit pulls ahead (≈0.244 vs 0.167). The phase transition is real and itemization-gated.

Insight 04 — Crit and crit-damage are multiplicative

The cross-term c·g means crit chance and crit damage amplify each other, and both ride the auto block that attack speed enlarges. Crit is therefore the keystone of a committed auto build — and nearly worthless as a splash stat on a burst combo.

06 — Mitigation

The penetration stack & effective armor

League applies armor modifiers in a strict order, and that order — not raw magnitude — decides whether lethality or percent penetration is better against a given target.

Effective armor (penetration applied in order)
$$ R_{\text{eff}} = \max\!\Big(0,\; \underbrace{R}_{\text{base}}\cdot\underbrace{(1-\rho)}_{\text{1. \% reduce}}\cdot\underbrace{(1-\pi)}_{\text{3. \% pen}} - \underbrace{\Lambda}_{\text{4. lethality}}\Big) $$

Lethality subtracts after the percentages have already shrunk armor. The marginal value of each pen type therefore depends on where the target sits:

Insight 05 — The crossover sits near R_eff ≈ 100

At 100 armor, 1 point = 1% effective HP (per the wiki), so flat and percent behave comparably. Below it, flat lethality dominates (it can drive R_eff to literal zero on squishies); above it, percent pen removes far more absolute armor. Gold prices (lethality 30g vs %pen ~42g/pt) reinforce the same break.

Optimal policy

Buy AD/lethality continuously — both deliver guaranteed value on every cast because Quinn's combo is ability- and on-hit-heavy. Treat attack speed as a lumpy breakpoint (only at risers, and her W steroid usually pays it). Pivot to crit only after an IE-tier crit-damage item makes the auto block large enough to justify it. Switch lethality→%pen as the enemy frontline's R_eff climbs past ~100.

08 — Keystone selection

Electrocute vs. Press the Attack

The two keystones interact with the combo in opposite ways. Electrocute is a flat one-time burst that ignores your scaling. Press the Attack is a smaller burst plus a multiplier on everything after its trigger.

Electrocute — flat additive (proc damage, no crit)
$$ \Delta D_{\text{Elec}} = \big(E_0(L) + 0.10A\big)\cdot\frac{100}{100+R_{\text{eff}}}, \qquad E_0(L)=70+11.18(L-1) $$
Press the Attack — burst + amp on post-trigger combo
$$ \Delta D_{\text{PtA}} = \Big(P_0(L) + (\alpha-1)\cdot D_{\text{post-trigger}}\Big)\cdot\frac{100}{100+R_{\text{eff}}}, \quad P_0(L)=40+8.24(L-1),\ \alpha\in[1.08,1.12] $$

For this build → winner

Electrocute

The combo is a one-rotation delete. Electrocute's flat burst lands fully and immediately; a target that dies in one combo never lives to benefit from PtA's lingering 6-second amp. The wiki itself frames Electrocute around exactly this 3-ability window.

When PtA wins instead

Press the Attack

Late-game crit/AD builds in extended fights, and repeated lane trades. PtA's amp grows with your combo size, compounds with crit (it multiplies the auto block too), and persists 6s for follow-up autos.

Insight 06 — Burst rewards flat, sustain rewards multiplicative

Electrocute adds a constant; PtA adds a percentage. A constant wins when your base combo is small or the fight is one rotation. A percentage wins when your combo is already large and the fight lasts. The "burst vs. sustain" axis is the "flat vs. multiplicative" axis.

09 — Synthesis

Conclusions

Six insights, one through-line: Quinn's combo is structurally ability- and on-hit-weighted, which makes guaranteed flat stats beat conditional multiplicative ones until the auto block is deliberately built up.

The optimal burst build, derived

AD/lethality core (guaranteed value every cast) → IE-tier crit-damage item to unlock the auto block → crit chance to scale → attack speed only to hit a clean breakpoint, largely supplied by Heightened Senses → Electrocute as keystone → lethality vs. % pen chosen per enemy frontline armor. Everything follows from the master equation in §03.

Model limitations

Three honest caveats. (1) Quinn's E armor-reduction lands mid-combo, so the first auto and Q technically hit pre-debuff armor — the single global mitigation factor slightly understates early hits. (2) The +1 immediate-auto and perfect animation-cancel assumption is best-case; lost autos shift AS breakpoints later. (3) PtA's amped fraction was approximated at 60% post-trigger; exact Harrier timing moves the keystone lines closer. None of these overturn the directional conclusions.
